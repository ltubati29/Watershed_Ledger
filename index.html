Watershed Ledger

Still water and flowing water need different questions. A pond holds on to what enters it. A creek carries it away and hands it to the next station. Each station is read by its own rules, then explained for people, pets and wildlife, and the stream.

Still water: ponds, lakes, reservoirs

Water stays for months or years. It layers by temperature, oxygen runs low near the bottom, and blooms build. One bad reading counts heavily, and bloom signs come first.

Flowing water: creeks, rivers, outfalls

Water is replaced in days and picks up oxygen on the way. Trouble arrives as pulses after rain, so spikes matter, and so does who is downstream.

    2. Documented event: Toledo, Ohio, August 2014

    A bloom in western Lake Erie reached the city's water intake. Public records report toxin in treated water above the WHO guideline of 1 µg/L, and more than 400,000 people were told not to drink their tap water. Below, the app's toxin rule is applied to the published values.

    What this shows: the rule flags every published reading above the guideline. What it does not show: early warning. That needs the days of data before the event, which these sources do not give. Lake toxin inside algal cells is a warning sign, not the same as tap water.

    Log a reading

    No kit? Pick what you can see. A band is saved as its middle value and tagged as a citizen reading. Anything you leave unpicked keeps its current value. Flow under 0.05 m/s counts as still water.

    Import readings (sensor feeds and files)

    Paste CSV with a header row: name,lat,lon,flow,ecoli,cyano,do,temp,ph,turb,cond. Names match existing sites or add new ones (up to 12). Comma, tab or semicolon separated all work. A blank cell means not measured: an existing site keeps its value, and a new site leaves it out of the score. The companion script fetch_usgs_to_ledger.py turns USGS sensor values into this format.

    Or add your own station

    Export in standard formats

    FHIR-style output is a Bundle of R4-shaped Observation resources, each with value, UCUM unit where one fits, time, position and data source. It is not validated against a FHIR server, and measure codes use a local system until mapped to LOINC. The CSV matches the import format, so it round-trips.

    What is real, and what is not

    Real: the Potomac station readings, pulled from the USGS Water Data API on 1 Oct 2026 and marked provisional by USGS. Not measured: bacteria, blooms and flow speed at the Potomac station, so the app says "not measured" and does not guess.

    Not yet validated: the scoring thresholds are starting points from public guidance, and the Toledo check is a consistency check, not a prediction test. A fair next test is NOAA's published western Lake Erie monitoring record: check whether the app would have flagged conditions days before August 2014. We also tested the Kaggle "Water Pollution & Disease" file and set it aside: its values look simulated and it is not a live feed.

    Live refresh runs through a small local server (server.py), which calls the USGS API for you and saves data/readings.csv. Without it, paste CSV into Import. Map tiles are not used: the map is drawn from coordinates.
